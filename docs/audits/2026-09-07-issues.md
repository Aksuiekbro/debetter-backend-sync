## Confirmed new issues and fixes

### #14 — results publication must not hide tournaments or invitations

The reviewed local design explicitly says `disabled=true` only withholds outcomes. Main treated it as whole-tournament hiding; an uncritical merge also left invitation database filters and participant service guards enforcing the obsolete behavior.

The integrated behavior is:

- Tournaments, ordinary reads and accepted memberships remain discoverable whether results are published or hidden.
- Organizer and participant sent/received invitation pages include these tournaments; valid invitations can be created/reopened/accepted. Invitee ownership and terminal-state checks still apply.
- Public match/round responses retain pairings and completion, publish winner flags only when results are enabled, and always withhold exact scores. EDIT/FULL organizers retain exact result access.
- Teams and participants list/detail APIs also redact preliminary/speaker scores for non-editors, including nested team members. Score-range filters and score-based roster sorting are rejected for non-editors so private scores cannot be inferred from filtered results.
- Independent review found a matching sort bypass in match lists. Public match sorting now allows only `id`, `startTime`, `location`, `completed`, and `isBye`; private/nested score, contact and winner sorts are rejected before fetching matches. Editors retain valid result sorting.
- Round mapping no longer chooses result visibility implicitly. The round controller uses the same explicit result policy as the match controller, while preserving the main `Round.forView` nested participant/user fetch graph and unpublished-pairing protection.

Coverage: tournament discovery/membership tests, organizer invitation persistence and state-transition tests, participant invitation MockMvc ownership/acceptance/repeat-action tests, match and round publication tests, public/organizer roster and sort/filter tests for both publication states. Main's null-ballot 400 and LD participant-name read with Open-in-View disabled remain covered.

### #15 — disqualification must apply to regeneration and advancement

`RoundService.generateMatches` previously trusted the round's stored entrants. Disqualifying after those entrants were saved did not prevent later regeneration. Elimination winner extraction likewise ignored the qualification flag.

- Filter disqualified teams out of team-format generation and their members out of LD generation.
- Reject empty or incomplete eligible fields with descriptive 400s; no eligible entrant is silently dropped to complete a bracket.
- Reject advancement of a disqualified team or a solo winner from one, asking the organizer to correct the result or requalify. No replacement winner is invented.
- Keep historical matches unchanged when regeneration fails. Two actual HTTP integration cases have no surrounding test transaction: one remaining-incomplete field and one all-disqualified field. Both confirm the original match still exists and the round stays published after the 400, proving the service transaction rolls back its earlier clear/flush.

Coverage includes APF regeneration, LD candidate filtering, team/solo winner rejection, incomplete/empty eligible fields, and committed-state rollback checks.
